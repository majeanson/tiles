Three map-first directions

Arcade is out. These read as maps of somewhere real: the map runs off the edges into fog rather than stopping at a border, blocked ground is part of the terrain vocabulary, and beauty carries the mood instead of contrast. 2b is the nightmarish one. Boards are 390 × 844 at 46px per hex.

FOG AND THE ENDLESS EDGE Two tiers, both dark. Hard fog is everything you have never been adjacent to: flat near-black, no terrain information at all, the map simply unresolved. Soft fog is ground you have revealed but are not currently looking at: real terrain, dimmed to ~40%, desaturated and blurred 5px, so you can plan against it but not read fine detail. Placed ground under the camera is fully sharp. Revealed ground never reverts to hard fog: once seen it stays in soft fog permanently, so the map you have uncovered is a growing, permanent record and hard fog only ever shrinks. Blocked ground is remembered the same way, which turns it into a navigation landmark. The map scrolls: one-finger drag, pinch to zoom out to about 0.6× for planning, and a recentre control that snaps back to your active frontier. The camera moves over an endless field; the fog travels with the camera, not with the map.

Four separated values plus one texture direction each: crypt diagonal hatch, cemetery smooth, burial ground dotted, catacomb horizontal rule. Because the palette is muted, value spacing does most of the work — cemetery is the light one, catacomb the dark one, and the two mid-tones differ by texture axis. Blocked is the only cross-hatch on the board and the only pure neutral.
SIX-HEX CHAIN The ground gives way. First hex sinks and bleaches to bone white, then collapses inward with a puff of pale dust that briefly thickens the fog above it. 90ms per link, easing to 60ms, and each collapse pushes the fog line outward — so the cascade's real reward is that you can suddenly see further. Points rise as thin serif numerals and fade. Sound is soft and awful: earth falling, a wooden crack, breath.

The hard case, because vignetting eats contrast at the edges. WHAT THE THUMB REACHES Comfortable arc on a 390×844 device is roughly the bottom 300px, centre-weighted. So: cards live at 104–212px from the bottom, 88px tall (well over the 44px floor), and placement is two taps, never drag — pick a card, the board shows a ghost hex at the best legal cell, tap the board to move it or the card again to confirm. Dragging to the top third with one thumb is the thing that breaks; the top 120px of the board is display-only and the camera keeps the live frontier in the lower two-thirds.
THE POP CHOICE Full-screen, but it enters from the bottom and the two options occupy the bottom 45% — the cascade stays visible above, frozen at its final frame, because the choice should feel like spending what you just earned. Two panels, equal weight, plain words: more tiles / more points, each with one number under it. No timer, no third option, no confirm step.
HUD RULE The HTML chrome never animates except when a number changes. All motion belongs to the canvas. Four readouts, one row, no icons, no bars — and only tiles is allowed a colour, because it's the life total.
